### 1.2 The unifying lesson, and it has already cost two findings

> **For a term that moves a TARGET, the output that HOLDS the target is the wrong observable.**

`bankClampActivePct` is one instance. The other is **GENERALITY-REVIEW finding 16**, which called the
v0.78 `MarkerRateFeedForward` inert for twenty-one releases because roll stick did not move. It is
worth **55–58% of the standing azimuth error**. Roll stick is the servo output that holds a trimmed
bank; a correct servo holding a *shifted* target produces almost no extra deflection. The null was in
the instrument.

The cheap screen, now written into `R40-metric-repair.md` and finding 16: **before reading a metric as
evidence that a term is inert, check that the column is downstream of the term and upstream of the loop
that cancels it.** A signal on the far side of a closed loop reads ~0 whether the term did nothing or
worked perfectly.

R40-rotor found the same shape one gate further downstream: `bankClampActivePct` reads `bankTR`
(correctly, post-repair), which is computed **upstream** of `tBankE *= (1 − _heliBlend)`. At
`heliBlend = 1` the law deletes that demand entirely — measured `|bankTR|` p95 76–86° against a 72°
clamp while `|tBankE|` p95 is **0.00** — and the scorer stamped `rotor-hover/AttackHelo1/hoveryawR`

### 1.10 The AoA guard's onset varies 40% across the roster, from two absolute-degree constants

`R39-E` §5, and it is provable from source before any data is consulted.

```csharp
float aoaMargin = Mathf.Min(4f, 0.15f * lim);                  // :1216
float aoaFade   = Mathf.Max(4f, Mathf.Min(6f, 0.25f * lim));   // :1222
float aoaCeil   = lim - aoaMargin;                             // :1223
```

Under the unclamped proportional expression, `onset/ceil = (1 − 0.15 − 0.25)/(1 − 0.15) = 0.7059` for
**every** airframe. Measured across the roster it spans **0.529 → 0.739**, a 40% spread, entirely clamp
artefact. The proportional form is live only for `alphaLimiter ∈ [16, 24]`, and the roster is 10, 10, 10,
10, 14, 15, 15, 25, 27, 27 — **the proportional form runs on 0 of 10 airframes** (R40-stol §5.1; R39-E
says 0 of 8 on its narrower fleet). Seven on the floor, three on the cap. The fade the law actually uses,
as a fraction of each limiter: **40.0% at lim 10, 26.7% at 15, 24.0% at 25, 22.2% at 27 — a 1.8× spread
in a quantity the ONE-LAW rule requires to be constant or probe-derived.**

The mechanism is not inferred: the predicted bite-set matches the measured one **8/8**, and
`clamp01((ceil − |aoa|)/fade)` reproduces the recorded `gateMinUp` to **±0.024 on 7 of 8 lanes**. The
counterfactual is clean too — **remove both clamps and EW1 and Darkreach stop being gated at all**; two
of the five "limiter active" lanes are floor artefacts.

The cleanest single statement is the matched pair: **EW1 at 0.554 of its ceiling has its pitch authority
gated (`gateMinUp` 0.933); SmallFighter1 at 0.568 — effectively the same fraction — never moves its guard
(1.000). The only difference is that 4/10 is larger than 6/25.**

**The v0.61 comment is a deliberate design choice and it contains a factual error.** Its justification is
sound and physical — the overshoot the fade must exceed is `_aoaRateFilt × aoaLead`, a rate times a time,
which is in *degrees*, so an absolute floor is the right **shape**. But it is written as the constant `4f`
rather than as the lead overshoot the mod computes one line away, which is exactly the substitution the
ONE-LAW rule asks for. And its bolded claim — *"for lim ≥ 16 the floor is INACTIVE, so FS-12 (lim 27, fade
6) and every jet with lim ≥ 16 are byte-identical"* — is **false for lim > 24**: at 27, `Min(6f, 6.75)`
binds and `Min(4f, 4.05)` binds. Three of that card's eight lanes are governed by a clamp the comment says

### 1.11 More demand bought LESS AoA — and the corrected baseline inverts the recommendation

`R39-E` §3–§4, and this is the most counter-intuitive result of the day.

The brief stated that R35's `alpha-steps` returned `aoaAboveCeilingPct` = 0.0 on all 8 airframes at
8000 m / 250 m/s. **False.** It put **7 of 8 on the limiter and 2 of 8 past the ceiling** (Darkreach
`aoaPeakOverCeiling` 1.029, trainer 1.024). So the premise "8000 m / 250 m/s cannot reach the alpha regime"
is refuted, and the pre-authorised recommendation ("the card failed, not the law; the fix is lower q") was
not issued.

Because both cards flew the *identical* entry condition and only `alpha-sweep` missed, the difference is
**demand shape**. `alpha-sweep` commands **4–50× the turn rate** of `alpha-steps` and reaches a **lower**
`aoaPeakOverCeiling` on **7 of 8** airframes. Mechanism, measured: the aircraft rolls to the 72° bank
clamp, pulls what the turn-rate cap allows, and pays the deficit in **altitude** — 525–2428 m of descent
into denser air, so **q rises 3–32%** and the AoA needed for the same load factor *falls*. All 60 segments
are RAILED — bank clamp 74–97%, turn-rate cap 85–97%, blend rail 81–96% — and never on AoA.

**A demand derived from the g limit saturates the bank/turn-rate channel before the alpha channel can
load.** `deriveAzRate` builds its rate from `aircraftGLimit`, so an "alpha" card measures the wrong limit.

### 1.12 `oblique-6-dwell` is retired as a between-airframe ranking instrument

`R39-B`. Criterion B **FAILS and not narrowly**: throttle 0.40 holds within-capture speed drift under
1.15× on **3 of 10** airframes (needed ≥8) and still runs 1.55× (Multirole1) and 1.67× (Darkreach). t100
is worse on 10 of 10. Three independent reasons it is structural:

1. **At 0.40 the fleet already straddles** — CAS1 *decelerates* (0.96) while Darkreach gains 1.67× at the
   same pinned throttle. One scalar against ten constraints already in conflict.
2. **The three-throttle curve is monotone on 10 of 10 and extrapolates to nothing usable** — 7 of 10 would
   need **negative** throttle for zero drift.
3. **The card commands a descent and the descent is throttle-independent.** The four legs dwell at
   elevations 0, −4.24, 0, +4.24 — mean zero — so the demand *looks* altitude-neutral. It is not: "nose on
   the marker" at positive AoA is a flight-path angle of −α, so the mean flight path over the card is −α.
   `∫V·sin(α − el_cmd)dt` reproduces the measured altitude loss at **Pearson +0.997 across all 128
   captures**. Every lane loses 221–1194 m on **both** arms; at t040 that altitude supplies **41%
   (Multirole1) to 107% (Darkreach)** of the entire kinetic-energy gain. Cutting throttle raises α, which
   raises sink, which feeds speed back — the loop closes off the fix.

**R37 §4's attribution is refuted**: Spearman(T/W, drift) = **+0.14 / −0.36 / −0.02** on the three throttle
arms. Darkreach has the *lowest* T/W in the fleet (0.348) and the *largest* drift on all three throttles.
The predictor that holds on all three is **Vmax/Vcorner** at +0.84 / +0.83 / +0.68 — the speed headroom
between where the card pins entry and where the drag polar wants to sit, which is the right shape for a
transit-to-trim. (Not identified: n=10, and it cannot be separated from mass. The *negative* result is the
safe one — nobody may cite T/W for this card's drift without re-deriving it.)

legs at t040 and 1.09–1.61× at t100.** Every "down leg vs up leg" statement built on this card family

The drift is a *transit to trim* and it completes within the capture: leg4/leg3 = 0.90–1.04 at t040 and

### 1.13 The Darkreach azimuth mode is real, V-dependent — and not the settle loop's

`R39-C`. The pre-registered numeric prediction **passes on all four legs**: Δf = +0.059 / +0.058 / +0.079 /
+0.065 Hz, all above the 0.04 threshold, all monotone with V. Darkreach is **32/32** with its autocorrelation
peak in the 0.25–0.50 Hz band at coherence 0.72–0.81; the other nine airframes are 3 of 480. Fitted
**f ∝ V^0.305 ± 0.015** pooled over four batches (n=72), **0.356 ± 0.026** within R39 alone. Quote 0.30 ± 0.02
for the corpus and 0.36 ± 0.03 for the clean within-batch contrast; the spread between the arms' own fits is
real curvature approaching Vmax, so a single power law is a summary, not a model.

**But the inference the prediction was supposed to license is refuted.** The settle path *is* the bank command
at that frequency — it reproduces `tBankE = 8.00 × azErr` (7.94–8.08 on 24 legs) at |phase| ≤ 0.5° on 29 of 32
legs — and its **return** gain is **0.054–0.325, i.e. 10–25 dB of margin, and the margin IMPROVES with V**
(0.29 at 110 m/s → 0.055 at 283 m/s). The bank ripple accounts for **5.4–33%** of the observed `azErr`.
`kSettle`'s own crossover is `8g/V` = **0.044–0.11 Hz**, four to ten times *below* the mode and moving the
wrong way. **Do not schedule `kSettle` against V or q.** The comment at `:1841` is measured correct.

**Where the mode actually lives:** the nose heading rate implied by the observed `azErr` is **3.1–18.5×
larger than `g·bank/V`**, `aimRate` is identically 0.0000 (stationary marker — not stimulus), and `outY` sits
in phase with `azErr` at 0.16–0.17 stick/deg while `outR` leads by ~+39°. It is a lateral-directional /
yaw-channel mode on one heavy airframe, and `kSettle` is downstream of it. The discriminating batch is a
`YawAssistEnabled`/`YawAssistStrength` A/B on Darkreach at throttle 1.00.

R39-C is also where the wobble-detector defect was first diagnosed; R40 then rebuilt the detector and
**independently reproduced R39-C's numbers**: per-leg Δf +0.078/+0.059/+0.075/+0.069, every one of the 8 cells
within **0.012 Hz** of R39-C's hand-computed value, four-batch exponent **+0.305 ± 0.029** against R39-C's
+0.304 ± 0.023, and — the check that matters — **0 settled-window episodes across all 512 R39 legs** against
42 of 42 at `tSeg ≈ 2 s` before. That is the strongest validation in the session: two independent instruments
built by two agents agreeing to three digits on a physical mode while disagreeing completely about the
artefact.

### What a deploy changes about how the harness behaves

**v0.99.0 — fleet geometry. This changes the physical layout of every batch.**

1. **Lanes sit on a ring, not a line.** Lane *k* at azimuth `2πk/M` on a circle of `RingRadius(M, decks,
   spread)` around the observer, replacing `AbeamM + LaneM·k`. Motivation is measured: the floating origin
   follows the operator's camera, so the old 8→98 km spread **was** `origDist`, float32 grain at *d* is
   `d·1.2e-7` m, and `Aircraft.gForce` is a finite difference that multiplies it by 60. R35:
   r(`origDist`, `gJitterG`) = **0.948**, log-log slope 0.885. R39: far-lane replicate σ **1.50×** near-lane.
2. **Each lane heads outward along its own radius** — no shared `_laneRot`. A card translates ~31 km, so a ring
   on one shared heading smears straight back into a 16→47 km spread mid-card; radial headings make `|pos|` a
   function of *t* alone.
3. **`AbeamM` 8 km → 5 km**, `LaneM` unchanged at 6 km but now the **chord** between adjacent lanes.
4. **`Cfg.DroneAltDeckM` defaults to 3000**, splitting the fleet over two decks at `startAlt ± 1500 m`.
   **Read this before the next launch: a card declaring 4500 m will fly at 3000 and 6000 m, not 4500.** There
   is a warning line for it, but it is a behaviour change on every existing card. Set it to 0 to reproduce the
   plain single ring exactly. Deck assignment is a Latin-square diagonal `((k/A) + (k%A)) & 1`, not `k % 2`
   (which confounds deck with airframe for even roster lengths) and not `(k/A) & 1` (which confounds deck with
   azimuth sector).
5. **Predicted effect of the shipped default** at 16 lanes: ring radius 13.32 km, `origDist` **uniform**, i.e.
   between-lane spread → exactly zero against a prior mean of ~53 km spanning 8–98 km. Roughly **3.4× jitter
   reduction**. Packing scales with the spread: N=16 gives 15.38 / 15.32 / 13.32 / 7.84 km at spreads
   0 / 500 / 3000 / 6000 m.
6. **Analysis consequence:** cross-batch **per-lane** `origDist` comparisons against R39 and earlier are void.
   A pre-0.99 "lane 7" is a point on a line; a post-0.99 one is a bearing on a ring. Aggregate comparisons are
   fine — that is the point.
7. **Recorded, not fixed:** `EntrySpeedFlyable` is density-blind (sea-level Vstall vs a TAS write; true stall
   TAS at 6 km is 1.36×), and altitude decks are what make that reachable.

**v0.99.1 — five harness defects and two law decisions. Net flight behaviour is byte-identical to v0.99.0.**

**New capability, item by item:**

1. **A settled window that is derived per segment rather than chosen.** `settled_from(t, xs)` bins `|x|` into
   2 s rms bins and starts at the first bin within **one e-fold of that segment's own quietest bin**. `e` is the
   natural unit of an exponential decay, not a knob. Justified from data: over 1,120 R35/R36/R37 oblique legs
   the envelope decays exponentially from ~4 s with a pooled τ of **6.4 s**, and the rule lands at 6–17 s on a
   30 s leg, moving with each leg's own decay. R39-C's hand-picked `tSeg ≥ 10 s` is one point inside that
   spread.
2. **An amplitude-independent frequency that is NULL without evidence.** Autocorrelation first peak of the
   detrended settled window, cross-checked against an **independent** Hann-windowed DFT. Published only when
   the peak clears white noise at `3/√N`, the two estimators land in the **same DFT bin** (`≤ 1/T` — demanding
   better than the instrument resolves is asking for precision nobody has), and the window holds ≥ 4 periods.
